Visiting the Urban Resilience Hub
with Respect

A guide for non-Native visitors — and why taking time
to prepare is itself an act of respect

🌿

This Is Sacred Ground, Not a Museum

The Urban Resilience Hub is the physical home of work rooted in Indigenous knowledge, community care, and decolonial practice. It is a living place of relationship — not a site to observe from the outside. Arriving without preparation, without invitation, or without intention to contribute replicates the settler-colonial patterns this work is actively undoing.

⚠️

Patterns to Unlearn

  • Dropping in unannounced
  • Expecting to be taught without contributing
  • Treating the visit as sightseeing
  • Placing the burden of education on Hub stewards
  • Moving faster than relationship allows
🤝

Visiting with Respect

  • Come with invitation and clear intention
  • Arrive ready to work, not just to observe
  • Follow the leadership of Hub stewards
  • Bring humility and willingness to learn slowly
  • Let relationship come before access

Visiting Guidelines Are on the Way

Guidelines for non-Native visitors are being developed in collaboration between the Hub's stewards and the Decolonial Repair Network. This process honors Indigenous leadership and ensures that visits are meaningful for everyone — not merely convenient.

In Development — Your Interest Is Valued

How to Prepare While You Wait

1

Learn about the Great Plains Action Society and the work Sikowis Nobiss leads

2

Reflect on your own relationship to settler colonialism and what you bring to this space

3

Speak with those who have worked at the Hub — they can share what the experience taught them

4

Trust that the time spent waiting and preparing will make the eventual visit more meaningful
